Roof Inspection in Portland: A Checklist for Property Owners

A roof inspection should document what is actually on the roof and set priorities, not manufacture urgency. Use this checklist before, during and after the visit.

Is an inspection worth booking now?

Yes, if you saw a stain but the drip stopped, since water may still be in the roof system; if the roof is older and has never been reviewed; if a storm recently hit the neighborhood; if moss, loose granules or sagging are visible from the ground; or if you are deciding between repair and replacement. Findings are most useful before the rainy season starts.

Do you need an inspection or a certification?

A buyer, lender or insurer may ask about roof condition. An inspection is not the same document as a roof certification unless that service is arranged, so confirm which one you need before the visit. Roof certification for real estate is a separate service for property transactions.

Field covering

Shingles or panels across every slope, ridges, hips and eaves, including wind-exposed edges.

Flashing

Every penetration and wall transition: chimneys, plumbing vents, fan vents, skylights, dormers and roof-to-wall joints. Most Portland leaks start at a penetration, not in the middle of a clean field of shingles.

Water paths

Valleys, gutters and downspouts, especially after needle and leaf drop from firs and maples.

Roof edge

Fascia, soffit and drip edge, where overflow shows up as peeling paint or soft wood.

Underside

Visible attic or underside clues when access exists and is safe: staining, damp insulation, daylight.

History

Signs of prior repairs, mismatched materials and repeated sealant, which suggests the source was never corrected.

Safe access

Wet, steep or brittle roofs may limit walking. Interior and edge observations can still be useful, and no one should pressure-wash or walk a wet roof to get a better look.

What you should receive

Observed conditions, not a vague claim that the roof is fine: photos showing the location of each finding, a list that separates urgent defects from maintenance items and monitoring points, and a plain answer on whether repair or replacement is the more practical path. Inspection and repair may be separate steps with separate written scopes.
